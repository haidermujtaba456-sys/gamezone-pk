PC Performance Optimisation: Make Your Games Run Smoothly

Performance GuideBy the GameZone PK Editorial TeamNovember 2026

A practical, step-by-step guide to faster loading, stable frame rates, and a cooler, quieter PC — without buying new hardware.

A slow game is rarely fixed by buying new hardware. In most cases, a few practical changes make a bigger difference than an expensive upgrade, and they cost nothing. This guide walks through those changes in order, from the simplest to the most involved, so you can stop at the point where your games already run well. The goal is a smooth, stable experience, not the highest settings on a chart.

Start with the basics. Update your graphics drivers, because new drivers regularly fix performance problems and improve stability. Then close the apps you are not using: a browser with many tabs, a video call, and a game running at the same time will slow each other down. Restart your computer regularly, because a fresh start clears memory and background tasks that build up over days. These three steps alone fix a surprising number of performance complaints.

Next, adjust your game settings with purpose. Resolution has the largest effect on performance, so if a game stutters, lower the resolution before touching anything else. Then reduce shadows and effects, which are expensive and hard to notice during play. Use the frame-rate limit to match your monitor, because rendering far more frames than your screen can show wastes power and creates heat. Test one change at a time, play for a few minutes, and keep the setting that feels smooth.

Storage and temperature matter too. Games on a fast drive load quicker and stutter less, so move your most-played games to the fastest drive you have. Keep air vents clear, clean dust from fans occasionally, and make sure your laptop is on a hard surface, not a bed or a cushion. A hot computer slows itself down to protect itself, so keeping it cool is a real performance improvement. Finally, remember the browser: close extra tabs before playing browser games, and keep your browser updated. Small habits, applied consistently, keep any PC running well for years.

Once the basics are done, a few extra checks can help even more. Check which programs start automatically when your computer turns on, and disable the ones you do not need — they slow every session, not just gaming. Keep at least ten percent of your main drive free, because a nearly full drive slows everything down. If you use a laptop, check its power settings and choose a performance mode while gaming, then switch back to a balanced mode afterwards to save battery. For browser games, try a clean browser profile with no extra extensions, because some extensions slow pages down without you noticing. Finally, keep a simple maintenance routine: once a month, update your software, clear temporary files, and check that your fans are clean and your vents are clear. None of this takes long, and together these habits keep a PC feeling fast for years. Performance is not a one-time fix; it is the result of small, regular care, and the players who practise it spend more time playing and less time waiting.
