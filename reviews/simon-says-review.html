Simon Says Review — A Memory Test in Colour

2026-11-26Reviewed by the GameZone PK Editorial Team★ 4.5 / 5

Simon Says is a memory and attention game built around colour and sound. Four coloured pads light up in a sequence, and you must repeat that sequence in the same order. Each round adds one more step, so the sequence grows longer and harder to remember. The game starts with a single colour and builds slowly, which makes it friendly for beginners, but after a few rounds it becomes a genuine memory challenge.

The gameplay rewards watching carefully and listening closely. Each pad has its own tone, so you can remember the sequence by sound as well as by colour — many players find it helps to say the colours out loud as they play. Breaking a long sequence into small groups also helps: remember the first three colours, then the next three, instead of trying to hold ten steps at once. There is no time pressure while you repeat the sequence, only the need to remember it correctly, which keeps the game calm even as it gets harder.

Presentation is bright and clear. The four colours are distinct and easy to tell apart, the tones are pleasant and different enough to recognise, and each pad lights up clearly when it plays. The feedback is immediate: you always know whether you pressed the right pad. Controls are a simple tap or click, and they work the same on desktop and mobile. The game runs smoothly everywhere, with no loading delays between rounds.

Simon Says is especially good for children and for anyone who wants to train focus and short-term memory in a playful way. It is also a relaxing game for adults: there is no opponent, no timer, and no penalty for playing slowly. Play regularly and you will notice your memory for sequences improving, one colour at a time. It is a gentle game with a real challenge hiding inside it.

The game also has a quiet benefit that is easy to miss: it teaches patience. There is no way to rush a sequence, no shortcut to remembering it, and no penalty for taking a breath before you repeat it. Children who play Simon Says learn to slow down and pay attention, and adults rediscover how much they notice when they stop multitasking. In a hobby full of fast, loud games, a slow, colourful memory game is a useful change of pace. It is also one of the few games that gets better when played slowly, which makes it a rare calm corner in a hobby that usually rewards speed.

Pros & Cons

Pros

Cons

Final Verdict
